# Inovers Journal — 90-Day Growth Plan

## The goal (be honest about it)

**By day 90:** 25,000 unique monthly readers, 800 signed-up members,
300 real comments across all posts, 1 organic external mention per week
(newsletter, podcast, press).

**Not the goal:** vanity traffic that bounces in 12 seconds, follower
counts, contests that give away merchandise, or paid promotion that
stops working the day the budget runs out.

## The strategy in one sentence

Publish one piece of writing a day that a thoughtful person would be
glad to have their name attached to, and put it in front of the exact
1,000 people who care about the intersection of *innovation*,
*creative thinking*, and *how India educates its children*. Everything
else follows from those two things.

## The reader we want

- **Primary:** Indian parents, teachers, principals, education founders,
  policy staff, curious students (17+), NGO leads. Age 22–55. Reads long-
  form. Has strong opinions on schooling. Actually shares things.
- **Secondary:** Innovators and founders in Bharat who are already on
  the inovers.in waitlist / Ideas platform.
- **Not the reader:** general Twitter timelines, listicle skimmers,
  outrage aggregators. We politely lose them by refusing to write for
  them.

## Editorial voice — the non-negotiables

- **Strong. Never shrill.** Take positions. Say "we think X." Never call
  a group of people stupid.
- **Evidence, always.** Every stat, quote, and claim links to a source
  that says exactly that. If no source, don't publish the claim.
- **Bharat in the examples.** Global evidence is welcome, but the
  concrete example should land in an Indian street, school, or lab
  wherever possible. That is where our readers live.
- **Short paragraphs. Long ideas.** No one reads dense walls anymore.
  Give one clear idea per paragraph, but do not water the ideas down.
- **Say the quiet part out loud.** If most readers privately agree with
  a critique but no one says it publicly, that's usually a good post.
- **No AI slop.** Auto-published looks like auto-published. See
  `../daily-research/SPEC.md` — the bot drafts, the editor publishes.

## The publishing rhythm

- **Day 0 launch:** 3 seed posts (the ones shipped in `content/seed/`).
- **Days 1–14:** one post/day, all editorially hand-selected from the
  research-bot queue. Editor's job is to reject aggressively — better
  to skip a day than publish a mediocre piece.
- **Days 15–30:** one post/day, plus one *guest voice* per week
  (teacher, parent, student, founder) commissioned by the editor.
- **Days 31–90:** one post/day + one guest/week + one long-form deep
  dive per month (~3,500 words, always human, always with original
  reporting).

## The 4-4-2 distribution mix

For **every** post, do 4 evergreen actions, 4 cadenced actions, 2
optional experiments. If any post skips this, you're just publishing
into a void.

### The 4 evergreen (do on every post, automated)

1. Sitemap includes the post → Google indexes.
2. RSS feed includes the post (`/api/rss`).
3. OG image + Twitter card generated → previews look good.
4. Auto-cross-post link fires to the mailing list.

### The 4 cadenced (do within 24 hours of publish, manual)

1. **WhatsApp share pack.** Editor writes a 2-line summary + link. Posts
   to 3 pre-agreed groups: (a) Teachers' network, (b) India Startup
   Ecosystem community, (c) Parenting community. Zero spam, one post
   per group per day max.
2. **Twitter / X.** Thread (5–7 tweets) that summarizes the piece.
   Include one striking pull-quote. Link at the end. Reply to the
   thread with the link again (algorithm hack).
3. **LinkedIn.** One long-post that stands alone as an idea, then a
   link to the full piece. Speaks to founders, edu leaders, and
   HR/talent.
4. **One relevant subreddit / one relevant HN thread.** For education
   pieces: r/india, r/IndianTeachers, r/education (US), sometimes HN.
   For AI pieces: r/singularity, sometimes HN. Only post where the
   piece genuinely fits — being marked spam once destroys the account.

### The 2 optional experiments (per month, not per post)

1. **DM to one journalist / editor** who has written on the topic.
   Not "please cover this" — a genuine "you might like this" with
   one specific line about their piece. One in ten will read it. One
   in fifty will link.
2. **Cross-publish a shortened version to Substack / Medium** with
   a canonical link back to inovers.in/blog/... (SEO safe when done
   right).

## The keyword frontier (India-specific SEO)

These are our first 25 keyword targets. Each should have one dedicated
post in the first 90 days. All chosen because: (a) they have real
search volume in India, (b) the current top results are shallow, (c)

See `keyword-targets.csv` for the full list with volume estimates.
Highlights:

- "how to develop critical thinking in students india"
- "NEP 2020 problems"
- "Indian school system flaws"
- "how does AI affect creativity"
- "innovation examples in india 2026"
- "why Indian students memorize"
- "atal tinkering lab success stories"
- "STEM education India"
- "problem-based learning schools india"

Rule: never write a post *purely* for a keyword. Write for the reader,
then use the keyword as the post's slug + h1 + first paragraph if it
fits naturally. If it doesn't, drop the keyword.

## Community seeding — the invisible layer that matters most

The first 90 comments on a Journal don't come from ads. They come from
the editor personally asking 15 real people to read the first post and
comment. Do this. Every launch that skips it dies quiet.

**Week 1:**
- Personal WhatsApp to 30 friends who are teachers, parents, founders.
  "We started a Journal. Here's the first post. Would love your take
  in the comments — even a one-line reply."
- Post the first three pieces in one *carefully chosen* WhatsApp
  community per day. Editor stays in the thread to answer.
- Ask 5 respected voices (teachers, edu founders you already know) to
  write a guest piece for month 2. Give them the deadline now.

**Weeks 2–4:**
- Every commenter gets a reply within 24 hours. Every.
- Highlight the sharpest comment of the week in the next post's footer
  ("From last week's comments: …"). This makes commenting worth it.

**Days 30+:**
- Editor picks 3 questions a week from the comment section and does a
  Friday "Reader Question" post — 500 words, one answer, one open
  question back. This is Quora-adjacent behavior without becoming Q&A.

## Monetization (deferred, not forgotten)

Do not put ads on it. Ever. It kills the brand and returns pennies.

Instead, from day 60:

- Small "Sponsored by Inovers Accelerator" strip at the top of every
  post pointing to the accelerator page (self-serve — no external ad
  network).
- Newsletter has a "Get funded" section once a month linking to /apply.
- School / district / govt inquiries via a "Book a session" CTA on
  education-tagged posts, converting into leads for the Edu OS
  product.

The Journal is a *lead engine* for the accelerator and Edu OS. That is
the whole business model.

## What "success" looks like at each milestone

| Day | Readers/mo | Members | Real comments | Notable |
|-----|-----------|---------|---------------|---------|
| 7   | 1,000     | 40      | 15            | First 3 posts live, WhatsApp send done |
| 30  | 5,000     | 200     | 60            | First guest post, first HN front page attempt |
| 60  | 12,000    | 450     | 150           | First press mention |
| 90  | 25,000    | 800     | 300           | Newsletter to 800; one keyword ranking top 3 |

If we're at half these numbers at day 90, we've still built something
serious. If we're at zero, the writing isn't landing — rewrite the
positioning, don't buy ads.

## The one thing that will kill this

Publishing under-cooked pieces because "we haven't posted today."
Better a quiet Tuesday than a bad Tuesday. The editor's job is to say
no more often than yes.
